<p><b>The International System of Units, the SI, is the system of units in which</b></p>

<ul><li><b>the unperturbed ground state hyperfine transition frequency of the caesium 133 atom, Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>, is 9 192 631 770 Hz,</b></li><li><b>the speed of light in vacuum, <i>c</i>, is 299 792 458 m/s,</b></li><li><b>the Planck constant, <i>h</i>, is 6.626 070 15 × 10<sup>−34</sup> J s,</b></li><li><b>the elementary charge, <i>e</i>, is 1.602 176 634 × 10<sup>−19</sup> C,</b></li><li><b>the Boltzmann constant, <i>k</i>, is 1.380 649 × 10<sup>−23</sup> J/K,</b></li><li><b>the Avogadro constant, <i>N</i><sub>A</sub>, is 6.022 140 76 × 10<sup>23</sup> mol<sup>−1</sup>,</b></li><li><b>the luminous efficacy of monochromatic radiation of frequency 540 × 10<sup>12</sup> Hz, <i>K</i><sub>cd</sub>, is 683 lm/W,</b></li></ul>

<p>where the hertz, joule, coulomb, lumen, and watt, with unit symbols Hz, J, C, lm, and W, respectively, are related to the units second, metre, kilogram, ampere, kelvin, mole, and candela, with unit symbols s, m, kg, A, K, mol, and cd, respectively, according to Hz = s<sup>–1</sup>, J = kg m<sup>2</sup> s<sup>–2</sup>, C = A s, lm = cd sr, and W = kg m<sup>2</sup> s<sup>–3</sup>.</p>

<p>The numerical values of the seven defining constants have no uncertainty.</p>

## The second

<p class="def">The second, symbol s, is the SI unit of time. It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the caesium frequency, Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>, the unperturbed ground-state hyperfine transition frequency of the caesium 133 atom, to be 9 192 631 770 when expressed in the unit Hz, which is equal to s<sup>−1</sup>.</p>

<p>This definition implies the exact relation Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub> = 9 192 631 770 Hz. Inverting this relation gives an expression for the unit second in terms of the defining constant Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>:</p>

$$
1\ \mathrm{Hz} = \frac{\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}}{9\,192\,631\,770} \quad\text{or}\quad 1\ \mathrm{s} = \frac{9\,192\,631\,770}{\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}}.
$$

<p>The effect of this definition is that the second is equal to the duration of 9 192 631 770 periods of the radiation corresponding to the transition between the two hyperfine levels of the unperturbed ground state of the <sup>133</sup>Cs atom.</p>

## The metre

<p class="def">The metre, symbol m, is the SI unit of length. It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the speed of light in vacuum, <i>c</i>, to be 299 792 458 when expressed in the unit m s<sup>−1</sup>, where the second is defined in terms of the caesium frequency Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>.</p>

<p>This definition implies the exact relation <i>c</i> = 299 792 458 m s<sup>−1</sup>. Inverting this relation gives an exact expression for the metre in terms of the defining constants <i>c</i> and Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>:</p>

$$
\begin{aligned}1\ \mathrm{m} &= \left(\frac{c}{299\,792\,458}\right)\mathrm{s} = \frac{9\,192\,631\,770}{299\,792\,458}\,\frac{c}{\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}}\\ &\approx 30.663\,319\,\frac{c}{\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}}.\end{aligned}
$$

<p>The effect of this definition is that one metre is the length of the path travelled by light in vacuum during a time interval with duration of 1/299 792 458 of a second.</p>

## The kilogram

<p class="def">The kilogram, symbol kg, is the SI unit of mass. It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the Planck constant, <i>h</i>, to be 6.626 070 15 × 10<sup>−34</sup> when expressed in the unit J s, which is equal to kg m<sup>2</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, where the metre and the second are defined in terms of <i>c</i> and Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>.</p>

<p>This definition implies the exact relation <i>h</i> = 6.626 070 15 × 10<sup>−34</sup> kg m<sup>2</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>. Inverting this relation gives an exact expression for the kilogram in terms of the three defining constants <i>h</i>, Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub> and <i>c</i>:</p>

$$
1\ \mathrm{kg} = \left(\frac{h}{6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34}}\right)\mathrm{m^{-2}\,s}
$$

<p class="noind">which is equal to</p>

$$
\begin{aligned}1\ \mathrm{kg} &= \frac{(299\,792\,458)^2}{(6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34})(9\,192\,631\,770)}\,\frac{h\,\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}}{c^2}\\ &\approx 1.475\,5214\times10^{40}\,\frac{h\,\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}}{c^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<p>The effect of this definition is to define the unit kg m<sup>2</sup> s<sup>−1</sup> (the unit of both the physical quantities action and angular momentum). Together with the definitions of the second and the metre this leads to a definition of the unit of mass expressed in terms of the Planck constant <i>h</i>.</p>

## The ampere

<p class="def">The ampere, symbol A, is the SI unit of electric current. It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the elementary charge, <i>e</i>, to be 1.602 176 634 × 10<sup>−19</sup> when expressed in the unit C, which is equal to A s, where the second is defined in terms of Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>.</p>

<p>This definition implies the exact relation <i>e</i> = 1.602 176 634 × 10<sup>−19</sup> A s. Inverting this relation gives an exact expression for the unit ampere in terms of the defining constants <i>e</i> and Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>:</p>

$$
1\ \mathrm{A} = \left(\frac{e}{1.602\,176\,634\times10^{-19}}\right)\mathrm{s^{-1}}
$$

<p class="noind">which is equal to</p>

$$
\begin{aligned}1\ \mathrm{A} &= \frac{1}{(9\,192\,631\,770)(1.602\,176\,634\times10^{-19})}\,\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}\,e\\ &\approx 6.789\,6868\times10^{8}\,\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}\,e\,.\end{aligned}
$$

<p>The effect of this definition is that one ampere is the electric current corresponding to the flow of 1/(1.602 176 634 × 10<sup>−19</sup>) elementary charges per second.</p>

## The kelvin

<p class="def">The kelvin, symbol K, is the SI unit of thermodynamic temperature. It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the Boltzmann constant, <i>k</i>, to be 1.380 649 × 10<sup>−23</sup> when expressed in the unit J K<sup>−1</sup>, which is equal to kg m<sup>2</sup> s<sup>−2</sup> K<sup>−1</sup>, where the kilogram, metre and second are defined in terms of <i>h</i>, <i>c</i> and Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>.</p>

<p>This definition implies the exact relation <i>k</i> = 1.380 649 × 10<sup>−23</sup> kg m<sup>2</sup> s<sup>−2</sup> K<sup>−1</sup>. Inverting this relation gives an exact expression for the kelvin in terms of the defining constants <i>k</i>, <i>h</i> and Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>:</p>

$$
1\ \mathrm{K} = \left(\frac{1.380\,649\times10^{-23}}{k}\right)\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}}
$$

<p class="noind">which is equal to</p>

$$
\begin{aligned}1\ \mathrm{K} &= \frac{1.380\,649\times10^{-23}}{(6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34})\,(9\,192\,631\,770)}\,\frac{\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}\,h}{k}\\ &\approx 2.266\,6653\,\frac{\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}\,h}{k}.\end{aligned}
$$

<p>The effect of this definition is that one kelvin is equal to the change of thermodynamic temperature that results in a change of thermal energy <i>kT</i> by 1.380 649 × 10<sup>−23</sup> J.</p>

## The mole

<p class="def">The mole, symbol mol, is the SI unit of amount of substance. One mole contains exactly 6.022 140 76 × 10<sup>23</sup> elementary entities. This number is the fixed numerical value of the Avogadro constant, <i>N</i><sub>A</sub>, when expressed in the unit mol<sup>−1</sup> and is called the Avogadro number.</p>

<p class="def">The amount of substance, symbol <i>n</i>, of a system is a measure of the number of specified elementary entities. An elementary entity may be an atom, a molecule, an ion, an electron, any other particle or specified group of particles.</p>

<p>This definition implies the exact relation <i>N</i><sub>A</sub> = 6.022 140 76 × 10<sup>23</sup> mol<sup>−1</sup>. Inverting this relation gives an exact expression for the mole in terms of the defining constant <i>N</i><sub>A</sub>:</p>

$$
1\ \mathrm{mol} = \left(\frac{6.022\,140\,76\times10^{23}}{N_{\mathrm{A}}}\right).
$$

<p>The effect of this definition is that the mole is the amount of substance of a system that contains 6.022 140 76 × 10<sup>23</sup> specified elementary entities.</p>

## The candela

<p class="def">The candela, symbol cd, is the SI unit of luminous intensity in a given direction. It is defined by taking the fixed numerical value of the luminous efficacy of monochromatic radiation of frequency 540 × 10<sup>12</sup> Hz, <i>K</i><sub>cd</sub>, to be 683 when expressed in the unit lm W<sup>−1</sup>, which is equal to cd sr W<sup>−1</sup>, or cd sr kg<sup>−1</sup> m<sup>−2</sup> s<sup>3</sup>, where the kilogram, metre and second are defined in terms of <i>h</i>, <i>c</i> and Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>.</p>

<p>This definition implies the exact relation <i>K</i><sub>cd</sub> = 683 cd sr kg<sup>−1</sup> m<sup>−2</sup> s<sup>3</sup> for monochromatic radiation of frequency <i>ν</i> = 540 × 10<sup>12</sup> Hz. Inverting this relation gives an exact expression for the candela in terms of the defining constants <i>K</i><sub>cd</sub>, <i>h</i> and Δ<i>ν</i><sub>Cs</sub>:</p>

$$
1\ \mathrm{cd} = \left(\frac{K_{\mathrm{cd}}}{683}\right)\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}\,sr^{-1}}
$$

<p class="noind">which is equal to</p>

$$
\begin{aligned}1\ \mathrm{cd} &= \frac{1}{(6.626\,070\,15\times10^{-34})\,(9\,192\,631\,770)^2\,683}\,(\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}})^2\,h\,K_{\mathrm{cd}}\\ &\approx 2.614\,8305\times10^{10}\,(\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}})^2\,h\,K_{\mathrm{cd}}\,.\end{aligned}
$$

<p>The effect of this definition is that one candela is the luminous intensity, in a given direction, of a source that emits monochromatic radiation of frequency 540 × 10<sup>12</sup> Hz and has a radiant intensity in that direction of (1/683) W sr<sup>−1</sup>.
